Video calls

Kalends can make a new meeting, with its own link, for every booking on Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, Webex or Jitsi Meet. The link is shared only once a meeting is confirmed, and cancelling a booking deletes its Zoom, Teams or Webex meeting. Set up the services you use here, then choose which ones each audience can pick under Meeting requests on its page.

Zoom, Teams, Google Meet and Webex each need a free developer app that belongs to you, so no one else's server is involved. It takes about 10 minutes once; the steps are below each service. Jitsi Meet needs nothing at all. Prefer something simpler? A fixed link (your personal Zoom room, say) works without any of this.

How to set up Zoom
  1. Sign in at marketplace.zoom.us and choose Develop › Build App › Server to Server OAuth App. Name it “Kalends”.
  2. On App Credentials, copy the Account ID, Client ID and Client secret into the boxes above.
  3. Fill in the required Information fields (name, your email).
  4. On Scopes, add meeting:write:meeting:admin and meeting:delete:meeting:admin (older apps: meeting:write:admin).
  5. On Activation, click Activate your app. Then save here and use Make a test meeting.

If Zoom says the app can't be built, your account's owner or admin has to allow it (Account settings › Role management). On a free Zoom plan, meetings with 3 or more people end after 40 minutes.

How to set up Teams
  1. Go to entra.microsoft.com › App registrations › New registration. Name it “Kalends”; for account types choose Accounts in any organizational directory (or only your organisation's). No redirect URI is needed.
  2. Copy the Application (client) ID into the box above.
  3. Under Authentication, turn on Allow public client flows and save.
  4. Under API permissions › Add a permission › Microsoft Graph › Delegated, add OnlineMeetings.ReadWrite.
  5. Save here, click Sign in to Microsoft, and enter the code it shows.

With a school or company account, your IT department may have to approve the app before you can sign in (the sign-in page says “Need admin approval”). You can make the app registration with any Microsoft account, even if the meetings are on your school one.

How to set up Google Meet
  1. In the Google Cloud console, create a project (e.g. “Kalends”), then APIs & Services › Library › enable the Google Meet REST API.
  2. OAuth consent screen (Google Auth Platform): choose External, fill in the app name and your email. Under Audience, click Publish app so it's “In production”. (In “Testing”, Google signs you out every 7 days.) You don't need Google's verification for your own use.
  3. Clients › Create client › Web application. Under Authorized redirect URIs, add exactly:
    {{ redirect_uris.google }}
  4. Copy the Client ID and Client secret into the boxes above, save, and click Sign in with Google. Google warns that the app isn't verified: choose Advanced › Go to Kalends, since it's your own app.

Sign in from a device on your tailnet, because Google sends you back to the address above. If you reach the panel some other way, set it as the “Settings panel address” on Notifications and use that address here. Google Meet links can't be deleted, so a cancelled booking's link simply goes unused.

How to set up Webex
  1. Sign in at developer.webex.com with your Webex account, then open your avatar › My Webex Apps › Create a New App › Integration.
  2. Name it “Kalends”, pick any icon, and under Redirect URI(s) add exactly:
    {{ redirect_uris.webex }}
  3. Under Scopes, tick meeting:schedules_read and meeting:schedules_write, then Add Integration.
  4. Copy the Client ID and Client Secret (shown only once) into the boxes above, save, and click Sign in with Webex.

Webex's meeting API needs a Webex account with Meetings; if the test says your account can't schedule meetings, your plan doesn't include it. Like Google, sign in from a device on your tailnet. Kalends renews the sign-in weekly so it doesn't lapse.

Jitsi Meet

{{ state('jitsi') }}

Free and open source, with nothing to set up: each booking gets a new room with a long random name, which works as its lock. Guests join from a browser or the Jitsi app without an account.

{{ tools('jitsi') }}
Good to know about Jitsi

On the free meet.jit.si, the first person into a room becomes its moderator and has to sign in with Google, GitHub or Facebook, so join a few minutes early and sign in. Everyone else joins without an account. Many universities and organisations run their own Jitsi server without that step. Jitsi rooms can't be deleted, so a cancelled booking's room simply goes unused.
